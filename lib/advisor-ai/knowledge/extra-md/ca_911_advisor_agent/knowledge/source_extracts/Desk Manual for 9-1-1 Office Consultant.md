**1.1 California 9-1-1 Background**

**California’s 911 Environment (Excerpt from the Strategic Plan -2009)**

**Authority**

The authority and purpose of California’s 9‐1‐1 “system” is established in Government Code sections 53100 to 53120 known as the Warren 911 Emergency Assistance Act (or the Warren Act). Essentially its purpose is to provide effective delivery of the public’s 9‐1‐1 calls to the appropriate local agency Public Safety Answering Points (PSAPs) that answer and respond to local calls for emergency assistance. The Warren Act authorized the State to oversee the development and continuance of this system, including the setting, monitoring and enforcing of technical and operational standards.

**911 Calls**

Californians place approximately 25 million 9‐1‐1 calls annually. Two‐thirds of those calls are from mobile devices (e.g., cell phones) and one‐third are from traditional landline telephones. At present only about one percent of the calls are from Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) devices. All 9‐1‐1 calls are routed through the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN) switches and wireless or VoIP routers and databases to deliver the call and provide the caller’s telephone number and location information to the answering PSAP.

**PSAPs**

At present California has 481 PSAPs, of which 86 percent receive 9‐1‐1 calls directly and are known as “primary PSAPs”. The remaining 14 percent only receive 9‐1‐1 calls that are transferred to them from primary PSAPs, and are referred to as “secondary PSAPs.” 60 percent of the primary PSAPs are city police departments, 18 percent are county sheriffs, 7 percent are university police departments, and 6 percent are California Highway Patrol (CHP) answering points. 85 percent of the secondary PSAPs are local or State fire protection agencies, while 6 percent are emergency medical services (most local fire agencies also provide emergency medical response services). By call center standards, most PSAPs are small: 56 percent of all of the PSAPs have only one to three State funded workstations, while 90 percent have seven or fewer funded workstations.

**The CA 911 Branch**

The CA 9‐1‐1 Branch funds the statewide and local 9‐1‐1 networks and databases, and funds the 9‐1‐1 call answering systems at the PSAPs. The CA 9‐1‐1 Branch has a staff of 16 of which three‐quarters are engaged in ongoing payment authorizations for the costs above. According to the number of 9‐1‐1 calls that each PSAP receives, the State of California (State) determines the number of local 9‐1‐1 network trunks and call‐taker workstations that it will fund. Throughout California’s PSAPs there are 1,939 State funded workstation positions in 481 PSAPs.

**Funding**

Approximately 90 percent of PSAP costs are borne by the local agency. These costs are primarily for staffing and facilities. The allocation of the 9‐1‐1 network, database and call‐taking equipment to the PSAPs represents the remaining 10 percent, which is paid by the State. The funds paid by the State are collected by the telephone companies from their customers as a special 9‐1‐1 fee on their bills. All California landline, wireless and VoIP customers are charged the fee, which is a percentage of their intra‐state calling costs. The fee is statutorily set at not less than one‐half of one percent (0.50%) and not more than three‐quarters of one percent (0.75%). The current fee is the minimum 0.50%. The fees are remitted to the Board of Equalization (BOE) for deposit into the State Emergency Telephone Number Account (SETNA) of the California Treasury. The SETNA fund balance averages about $100 million. The SETNA funds are used to pay for the CA 9‐1‐1 Branch, the BOE’s 9‐1‐1 accounting activities, and for 9‐1‐1 network, database, call‐taking equipment, as well as related incidental expenses. Annual SETNA expenditures are about $100 million. Most of the money is paid to AT&T and Verizon, who are the principal providers of network, database and equipment.

**The 911 Advisory Board**

In 2005 a 9‐1‐1 Advisory Board was established to advise the CA 9‐1‐1 Branch on 9‐1‐1 issues and policy. The Board is made up of ten members and a chairperson. By statute the membership represents various public safety communities of interest (police chiefs, fire chiefs, National Emergency Number Association (NENA) and Association of Public Safety Communication Officers (APCO) representatives, etc). The chair is statutorily assigned to the Chief of the CA 9‐1‐1 Branch. Members are nominated to the Governor, who confirms the appointments. Board members are not paid for their time, but are reimbursed for travel costs. The Board meets quarterly. Its role is strictly advisory concerning a wide range of 9‐1‐1 topics.

**County Coordinators**

In order to effectively facilitate accurate 9‐1‐1 call routing, the CA 9‐1‐1 Branch has designated a 9‐1‐1 Coordinator in each county, statewide. The County Coordinator works with the 9‐1‐1 service and equipment providers, the PSAPs, and the

CA 9‐1‐1 Branch to ensure that accurate and timely delivery of 9‐1‐1 calls and related call information is accomplished. The role of the County Coordinators has evolved from primarily updating AT&T’s and Verizon’s Master Street Address Guide (MSAG) databases with ongoing changes, to also facilitating PSAP field training and coordination between 9‐1‐1 vendors and PSAPs. Most County Coordinators are employees of a county or of a PSAP. At least one County Coordinator is an independent contractor retained by a city. A few County Coordinators work full time in their County Coordinator role, while most perform other non‐County Coordinator functions. The State reimburses County Coordinators for their time spent on CA 9‐1‐1 Branch approved tasks. No County Coordinators in California are employees of the State.

**The 911 Industry and Associations**

California’s 9‐1‐1 services are comprised of a strong public‐private partnership. The private component is made up of a multitude of firms that provide and support 9‐1‐1 call delivery and call processing. The technical integration of these 9‐1‐1 industry providers for the processing of 9‐1‐1 services is quite complex. These industry partners are complemented by 9‐1‐1 industry and professional organizations such as the NENA and the APCO.

**2.1 9-1-1 Office Guidelines**

In order to ensure the best quality service to our customers and to maintain an efficient and professional office environment, please review and adhere to the office procedures below. Feel free to see your immediate supervisor if you have questions regarding this information. These policies apply to all office employees pursuant to applicable Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) agreements.

**Tardiness**

Tardiness in excess of 15 minutes shall normally be charged to Vacation or other appropriate leave type (e.g. Personal Leave, CTO, Annual Leave, etc.). Chronic tardiness or repeated unplanned absences may result in approved leave without pay (Dock) or an unapproved absence without leave (AWOL).

**Demeanor**

This office values and promotes a climate of mutual respect. As representatives of the CA 9-1-1 Branch, all employees are expected to interact with staff, vendors, clients and others in a courteous, respectful and professional manner.

**Rest Periods**

An employee may be granted a rest period on state time not to exceed fifteen (15) minutes each four (4) hours of his/her work shift, not to exceed 30 minutes each day. A rest period will not normally be granted during the first or last hour of the work shift. If an employee plans to leave the Division worksite (3 building campus) during the rest period, they must notify their supervisor. Rest periods may not be accumulated nor may they be used to “make up” time or extend lunch periods or absences.

**Meal Periods**

Full time employees shall normally be allowed a meal period of not less than 30 minutes, or not more than 60 minutes (for any shift exceeding 6 hours) and shall be scheduled near the middle of the work shift. Meal periods taken shall not be counted as part of total hours worked. Meal periods may not be accumulated nor may they be used to “make up” time or extend rest periods or absences.

**Planned Absences**

Approval or denial of a planned absence request will take into consideration such issues as office coverage, work commitments, leave time availability and/or business needs.

Planned absences are to be requested in writing, electronic mail being the easiest and most accessible means. Requests should be made a minimum of 24 hours in advance of taking vacation, annual leave, jury duty, doctor’s appointments and any other planned absence. Do not assume your absence is authorized if you have not received a written reply from your supervisor. Long term absences for periods of several days or weeks should be requested with as much lead time as possible. This process is necessary to plan workload and office coverage.

**Unplanned Absences**

Unplanned absences include all requests made less than 24 hours in advance. This includes sick leave and emergency requests for time off. The time off must be reported in writing as soon as possible upon return to the office, indicating the leave type and amount requested for the absence.

Unplanned absences must be discussed in person or by phone with your supervisor. An employee is required to call in regarding an unplanned absence no later than 30 minutes after their work start time.

When requesting an unplanned absence, be prepared to address the current status of your workload, backup coverage, and any business items needing to be addressed during your absence. These issues will be considered when approving or denying unplanned absence requests.

**Medical Substantiation**

Medical substantiation may be required for any medical absence. This includes sick leave, or other leave used in lieu of sick leave, in accordance with Memorandum of Understanding provisions. Medical substantiation must be signed by a treating doctor or licensed practitioner and must state the individual was unable to work due to illness/injury and may return to work on a given date. It should also address any restrictions. The office expects the substantiation to be provided within 72 hours of the request, unless you receive a written exception from your supervisor.

SUBSTANTIATION EXAMPLES

Acceptable:

*(Employee’s name) has received treatment for (nature of illness/injury) and was unable to work on the following date(s) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_. (Name) may return to work on \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (restrictions should be noted, if any).*

Unacceptable:

*1) Employee states he/she has been ill and unable to work. 2) Employee was given telephone advice on \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.*

**Absence without Pay**

APPROVED LEAVE WITHOUT PAY (LWOP) – An approved absence not using leave credits (commonly referred to as dock, shown in PAL as “L” – Dock, Informal Leave Granted).

ABSENCE WITHOUT LEAVE (AWOL) – Unapproved absence without pay (shown in PAL as “A” – Absence Without Leave). AWOL will be used when an employee did not report to work and/or:

* did not contact a supervisor or manager
* medical substantiation was required and employee failed to provide documentation by the end of the pay period
* was tardy without approval
* failed to get written approval

AWOL will also be used when an employee reports to work and leaves the job site without permission.

NOTE: An employee may not receive their State Controller’s paycheck on payday when absence without pay occurs and is reported after payroll cutoff.

**Overtime**

Requests to work overtime are to be submitted and approved by your supervisor in writing and prior to working overtime. Specific tasks to be accomplished should be detailed in the request to work overtime. After the overtime is worked, the employee certifies the actual hours worked, the supervisor verifies the tasks were completed, and the supervisor approves the actual overtime worked. (Use Form STD682 for request to work overtime.)

**Administrative Time Off**

Administrative Time Off is absence with pay for special circumstances, which may be granted by a supervisor. Some examples are for Division-sponsored luncheons, holiday parties, retirement parties/luncheons, picnics, or similar functions planned by specific groups or units within the Division which require more time than the employee’s normal lunch period. All other events extending into state work hours must be covered with employee CTO or vacation time. The employee must receive advanced written approval for this leave.

**USE OF BUSINESS TOOLS**

**Personal Use of Telephones**

State telephones are provided for state business and should not incur costs associated with personal use.

Policy regarding the use of state telephones for personal use is as follows:

* Personal long distance calls should not be placed from state telephones, except when arrangements are made with the long distance operator at the time of the call to directly bill the caller.
* The number and length of personal calls should be kept to a minimum and must not interfere with state business.

**Voice Mail**

For voice mailboxes, the personal greeting of the mailbox “owner” shall be updated daily and contain instructions for reaching a live attendant by pressing 0+3 or by hanging up and dialing 657-9369.

Employees are expected to respond as soon as possible, but no later than the following business day, to any new voice mail message where the caller requests information or acknowledgment, even if the request is unspoken but implied.

When employees know they will be unavailable to respond to messages or will be out of the office for an extended period of time, they must update their voice mail greeting to reflect the change and give a referral of someone to contact in their absence. After the employee has returned from their absence, the voice mail greeting should be updated.

**E-Mail**

Employees are authorized to use e-mail (including Internet access) for state business only. Users will not engage in any activities via e-mail that will in any way discredit the Department or State service.

* Ownership **-** All electronic mail messages composed (sent and received) are, and remain at all times, the property of DGS.
* Right to Retrieve **-** DGS reserves the right to retrieve and read any message composed, sent, or received. Ultimate privacy of messages cannot be guaranteed.
* Privacy -Except as indicated above, electronic mail is generally considered to be private and, as such, is to be read by only the addressed recipient or at the direction of the addressed recipient.
* Content -Electronic mail may not contain content that may be reasonably considered offensive or disruptive to any employee. Offensive content may include, but is not limited to, sexual comments or images, racial slurs, gender specific comments, or any comments that would offend someone on the basis of age, sexual orientation, religious, or political beliefs, national origin, or disability.
* Checking Inbox - Be sure to read your e-mail inbox messages at least once a day when you are in the office. Respond as soon as possible, but no later than the following business day, to any new inbox message where the sender requests information or acknowledgment from you, even if the request is unwritten but implied. Responses may be made either by e-mail, telephone, or in person.
* Absences - If you know you will be unable to read your e-mail messages for 24 hours or more, such as when on vacation or traveling, turn on the “Out of Office Notification” and give a referral of someone to contact in your absence. After you have returned from your absence, make sure you turn off the “Out of Office Notification”.

**Computers, Printers, & Copiers**

Computers, printers, and copiers are to be used for business purposes only.

* Computer games are not considered business programs and playing computer games is not considered a business activity for the CA 9-1-1 Branch. Therefore, computer games shall not be accessed, downloaded, and/or played on state provided computers, at any time.
* The use of color printers is authorized for business use **only when color is necessary**.

**3 9-1-1 Funding Policy**

**3.1 Methods**

Public Safety Answering points (PSAPs) requesting CA 9-1-1 Branch funds may use the reimbursement process or the direct funding process.

**3.1.1 Reimbursement**

The reimbursement funding process provides the PSAP with a vehicle to be compensated for 9-1-1 related expenses that are pre-approved for funding by the CA 9-1-1 Branch. Refer to *AGENCY* *9-1-1 EXPENSE POLICY* for services that many not need pre-approval. Invoices will be sent to and paid by the PSAP. The PSAP may then submit a Reimbursement Claim (TD-290) form to the CA 9-1-1 Branch.

**3.1.2 Direct Pay**

With prior approval from the CA 9-1-1 Branch, the direct pay process allows the PSAP to order the equipment and services and have all invoices sent directly to the CA 9-1-1 Branch. Direct funding requires a State of California procurement method, such as a State Master contract and/or CMAS (California Multiple Award Schedule).

**3.2 Allotment Calculations**

**CPE funding level calculations**

The CA 9-1-1 Branch has established CPE funding allotment amounts at various funding levels that are based on the quantity of 9-1-1 calls, including up to 20% of 9-1-1 calls that are abandoned before they are answered, and emergency 9-1-1 calls transferred to the PSAP by the use of non-published emergency lines. These 9-1-1 calls transferred to non-published lines may be added in the calculation for FUNDING LEVEL ONE, TWO, THREE and FIVE with the stipulation that the PSAP can offer documentation supporting the status of these 9-1-1 transferred 9-1-1 calls in a formal report with statistics collected from an alternate MIS that monitors the traffic electronically or Computer Aided Dispatch (CAD) incident reports. (FUNDING LEVEL FOUR accounts for transferred 9-1-1 calls in the formula provided). All funding level calculations for PSAPs with a call volume of over 1200 calls per month will be performed using the calculations for funding level four (typical Busy Hour) and funding level five (typical Busy Month). The calculation that provides the highest CPE funding amount for the PSAP will be applied.

**Customer Premise Equipment (CPE) Funding Policy**

It is the policy of the CA 9-1-1 Branch to ensure high quality and best value for agencies in the procurement and maintenance of 9-1-1 Customer Premise Equipment (CPE) and services. To accomplish this, the

CA 9-1-1 Branch staff will advise and support PSAPs in their efforts to procure 9-1-1 CPE that maximizes the ability of the PSAP personnel to efficiently and effectively answer and direct 9-1-1 calls. To receive

**3.4 GIS**

**Geographic Information System (GIS) Funding Policy**

In accordance with Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Order

94-102, the CA 911 Branch launched a project to implement Enhanced

9-1-1 (E9-1-1) services for wireless telephone users throughout California. A major emphasis of this project is the redistribution of statewide wireless 9-1-1 call volume to local PSAPs from the California Highway Patrol (CHP) communication centers that handle an overwhelming number of wireless 9-1-1 calls. As an incentive for PSAP participation in this project that will significantly improve 9-1-1 caller response time, the CA 9-1-1 Branch will provide participating PSAPs a one-time allotment for costs associated with implementation of GIS. Primary PSAPs that agree to answer wireless 9-1-1 calls directly or secondary PSAPs that receive transferred wireless calls through the 9-1-1 network may request GIS Funding by following the GIS Reimbursement process or the GIS Direct Funding process outlined in the 9-1-1 Operations Manual.

See page III.16 in the link below for the complete GIS Funding Policy

**3.4 9-1-1 Network**

**9-1-1 Network Services Funding Policy**

Every telecommunications carrier offering local voice network (wireline and wireless) in the State of California is required by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) to provide customers access to 9-1-1. Therefore it is the policy of the CA 9-1-1 Branch to provide cost recovery funding to telecommunications carriers for delivery of wireline and wireless 9-1-1 services in the State of California. Funding shall be contingent upon compliance with the NENA recommended standards; contingent upon compliance with the standards, policies and procedures defined by the CA 9-1-1 Branch; and based on the CPUP Tariff rates and State contracted rates.

**Eligible PSAP Network Services**

The following items are available for 9-1-1 Office funding:

10 Digit Emergency Number (unlisted) *(Reimbursement Claim Process)*

9-1-1 Telephone Line (trunks) *(Direct Funding Process)*

ALI Dial Back-up circuit (line) *(Direct Funding Process)*

ALI Retrieval Circuit *(Direct Funding Process)*

Alternate Answer Circuit *(Direct Funding Process)*

CPE Maintenance Telephone Line *(Direct Funding Process)*

Foreign Language Interpretation Services *(Contact CA 9-1-1 Branch Consultant)*

Management Information Services (i.e. FRNIS & CARSNet) *(Direct Funding)*

End office 9-1-1 trunks *(direct funding Process)*
