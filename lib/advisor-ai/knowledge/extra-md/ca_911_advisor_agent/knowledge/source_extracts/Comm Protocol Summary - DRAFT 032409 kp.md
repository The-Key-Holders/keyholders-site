**9-1-1 Office - Communication Protocol Summary**

Conflict is an inevitable part of work life. Direct communication among the parties involved is an effective and efficient place to begin the conflict resolution process. However without sufficient ground-rules the method can prove very challenging.

Following is an overview of the recommended process and personal etiquette required to successfully implement the direct communication method, and reap its potential benefits.

**Personal Etiquette**

* No retaliation.
* Be direct and honest.
* Come with an open mind.
* Be respectful. Don’t interrupt.
* Use a civil voice tone and language.
* Be aware that body language also factors.
* Acknowledge your contribution to the conflict.
* Attempt to understand another party’s perspective.

**Process Overview**

* Time: Mutually agree, specify duration.
* Location: Mutually agree, informal, private.
* Expectations:
* Confer and identify the general topic to discuss.
* Bring constructive alternatives to resolve the issue.
* Address the issue as an inquiry not a personal attack.
* Have dialogue and each party express their perspective.
* Take others concerns seriously. Ask clarifying questions.
* Acknowledge others ideas by summarizing or paraphrasing.
* Break as needed to consider info discussed before responding.
* Summarize conclusions. Agree on one another’s responsibilities.
* Implement the specific detail agreed on in an effort to resolve issue.
* Jointly escalate to next level if unresolved. Mediation using protocol.
* Mediator retains authority to decide/escalate issue if still no agreement.

**Objectives**

* Resolve conflicts at the lowest level possible.
* Increase productivity by resolving issues efficiently.
* Structured process all are familiar with and have agreed to.

**Reminders**

* Don’t fuel-the-fire when told about someone else’s issue with another person.
* Refer to the protocol, and suggest concerned parties discuss issues directly.
* Not intended to address severe issues like harassment or discrimination.
* Does not modify or replace the existing formal complaint processes.
